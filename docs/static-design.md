# The browser reading room

The brief explicitly asks for warm paper and an editorial reader. Newsreader
sets book titles and source text; Source Sans 3 sets controls. Latin/Spanish font
subsets and their SIL OFL notices ship with the app. No font CDN is called.

Paper, sepia and night themes share CSS tokens for text, surface, accent, status,
focus and spacing. The accent is restrained copper in daytime and amber at night.
Text sits in a readable column. The shelf uses actual EPUB covers or deterministic
lettering covers. A bookmark ribbon shows the saved reading position.

Preparation is visible in the book text. Unprepared sentences are graphite;
ready sentences have full ink contrast; the sentence whose audio is playing has
a warm highlight. A short ready run shows the buffer ahead of the playhead.
On desktop, contents and the player flank the reader. On phones, contents become
a keyboard-trapped drawer and the player remains thumb-reachable at the bottom.

Native dialogs trap focus and restore the triggering control. Labelled controls,
visible focus, aria-live progress and keyboard help support reading without a
pointer. Reduced motion disables transition/animation effects. Download states
show sizes and an action: download, pause, resume, cached or remove. Errors include
an applicable fix action. Inferred names and similarities are labelled; unknown
sections remain unclassified and all search results cite the document.

The frontend-design and Unslop guidance supplied by the user informed the final
review. The paper palette is a requirement of this brief. Book chapters provide
meaningful numbering; decorative statistics and stock SaaS cards are avoided.
The app explains actual privacy/storage limits in plain language.

Screenshots are in [screenshots/static](screenshots/static). Automated accessibility
checks and Lighthouse results are reported separately; they do not prove complete
screen-reader usability or speech quality.
